## 1. The rule the poem states

Line 8 makes the orthography of the feminine ending an explicit subject of study:

> مِنْ كُلِّ مَقْطُوعٍ وَمَوْصُولٍ بِهَا  ✽  وَتَاءِ أُنْثَى لَمْ تَكُنْ تُكْتَبْ بِهَا

and the poem devotes a whole chapter (باب التاءات, lines 94–100) to the words in which the feminine ending is written **open** (ت) in the Uthmanic rasm where ordinary spelling would close it (ة).

## 2. Why this is a bit and not a spelling quirk

The ending is genuinely bistable in performance: stopping on it realises it as hāʾ, continuing realises it as tāʾ. Two readings, one grapheme:

```
waqf  (pausal)      ة → ه =   5     state bit S0
waṣl  (continuous)  ة → ت = 400     state bit S1
                    ----------------
                    stride  = 395
```

**Every** state-bearing word therefore has two addresses exactly **395** apart. A word carrying *k* such graphemes is a *k*-bit register.
