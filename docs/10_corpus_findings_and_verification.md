## 3. Findings

### F1 — الْعَدَدْ weighs 109

The colophon states the count as قاف وزاي = 107. Its own final word, الْعَدَدْ, weighs 109: ا(1)+ل(30)+ع(70)+د(4)+د(4). The number stated and the number transmitted are both present in one hemistich. Only two words in the entire poem weigh 109 — الْعَدَدْ and قَطٍ, the latter being the abjad spelling of 109. (Arithmetic only; authorial intent is not claimed.)

### F2 — the paradox is positional, not arithmetical

The two collated witnesses carry the same closing lines in different order. Where the colophon stands last it reads as a false statement about a 109-line poem; where it stands at ordinal 107 it is true of its own position and the doxology follows as an appendix. The contradiction is manufactured by the splice point. See doc 02 §5.

### F3 — the feminine ending is a uniform-stride bit field

All 13 words of باب التاءات show Δ = 395 between their closed and open realisations, without exception, because the difference is always ت(400) − ه(5). The corpus carries 30 such bits over 24 lines. Uniform stride is what makes the state bit safe to put in the address.

### F5 — the heaviest line is the ظاء inventory

Line 54 weighs 11758, more than 15× the lightest. It is the line enumerating words containing ظ (weight 900), so the weight is carried by the subject matter itself — the poem's heaviest line is heavy *because* it is about the heaviest letter.

> ظَاهِرْ لَظَى شُوَاظِ كَظْمٍ ظَلَمَا  ✽  أُغْلُظْ ظَلامَ ظُفْرٍ انْتَظِرْ ظَمَا

The lightest, line 2, is the doxology — short words, light letters:

> الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ وَصَلَّى اللَّهُ  ✽  عَلَى نَبِيِّهِ وَمُصْطَفَاهُ
